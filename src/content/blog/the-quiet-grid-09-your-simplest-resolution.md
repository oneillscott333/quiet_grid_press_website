We are back, rested, and grateful. The solution to Issue 8's slow puzzle is on the site, and your quietest-moment sentences from December are the loveliest thing we have ever printed. Thank you. Now, a new year and a new rung.

## Three squares

**One.** A hidden pair. In one row, column, or box, find two digits that can each go in only the same two squares, and nowhere else in that unit. Those two squares are spoken for: they must hold those two digits, in one order or the other, and every other pencil mark in them can be erased. Nothing gets placed yet. Something gets cleared, and clearing is how the next placement appears.

**Two.** A blank log page in January is pure possibility. So is a sharpened pencil. So is the first grid of the year, which is waiting below.

**Three.** Behind the grid: the log book is here. *The Solver's Log* is a small, well-made book for writing down every puzzle you finish, from any source: date, where it came from, its difficulty, how long it took if you care to say, and three honest words about how it went. Every 25 entries, a checkpoint page asks a gentle question. At the back, a year-view grid of 150 squares fills in as you go. We made a free page of it to print, if you would like to try before you buy. Your simplest New Year's resolution: write down what you finish.

## One grid

In box 2 of this grid (top row of boxes, middle), two digits can only go in the same two squares. Which two digits, and which two squares?

```
9  1  . | 2  4  6 | .  .  3
.  .  . | a  .  . | .  .  1
3  .  . | b  .  8 | .  .  .
--------+---------+--------
6  .  3 | 8  .  . | .  .  .
.  9  5 | .  7  . | .  .  .
.  .  . | 3  5  1 | .  7  .
--------+---------+--------
4  .  . | .  .  7 | 6  .  5
.  .  9 | .  .  5 | .  .  .
.  .  . | .  2  . | 4  .  .
```

Box 2 is missing 1, 3, 5, 7, and 9. Squares a and b, both in column 4, have several candidates each. But two of the missing digits have no other home in the box than a and b. Find those two digits, and you may erase everything else from those two squares. Which two?
