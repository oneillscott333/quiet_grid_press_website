## Three squares

**One.** Before you touch a pencil mark, scan. Take each digit, 1 through
9, and sweep the whole grid for it once: where is every 4? Which rows,
columns, and boxes already have one? Most easy puzzles surrender entirely
to a patient scan, and the habit trains your eye for everything that
comes later.

**Two.** A puzzle you finish in one sitting is a small, complete story.
Beginning, middle, and the satisfying last square. Not every evening has
room for a novel. Most evenings have room for a story.

**Three.** Behind the grid: what "Easy" promises at Quiet Grid Press. On
our covers it is a promise, not an apology. Every Easy puzzle we publish
has been checked to have exactly one solution and a recorded logical path
with no guessing, and the difficulty does not quietly climb halfway
through the book. The puzzle you open at bedtime plays as fair as the one
you opened with breakfast. We call that a fair fight.

## One grid

This time, a whole grid, but only one square matters: the one in the very
center, row 5, column 5.

```
.  6  5 | .  4  . | 1  .  .
9  .  1 | 6  5  . | .  4  3
.  .  4 | .  .  . | .  .  9
--------+---------+--------
.  .  . | .  .  . | 3  .  .
6  .  3 | .  ?  2 | .  7  .
2  9  8 | .  3  5 | 6  1  .
--------+---------+--------
.  8  6 | .  9  . | .  .  .
.  3  . | .  8  . | .  .  .
.  .  . | 3  .  . | .  .  .
```

Look at that center square's row, its column, and the box it sits in.
Between the three of them, eight digits are already spoken for. Which one
is left? Solvers call this the last possible digit, and it is the
second-easiest move in Sudoku, right after the full house.
