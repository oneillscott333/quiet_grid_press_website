Issue 3's lonely candidate lived in row 5, column 6, and it was a **2**. A tip of the cap to the quick eyes. Now for the sneakier cousin of the naked single.

## Three squares

**One.** A hidden single hides among candidates. Pick a digit and a unit, say a 7 and a column. Look at every empty square in that column and ask: can a 7 go here? If a 7 already sits in that square's row or box, the answer is no. Very often, every empty square gets a no except one, and that one square must be the 7, even if its pencil marks show two or three other possibilities. The digit was hiding in plain sight. Lovely, is it not?

**Two.** The quiet of a house after everyone has gone to bed. Dishes done, one lamp on, the refrigerator humming its one note. It is the best solving light there is, and it costs nothing.

**Three.** Behind the grid: every puzzle we make has a permanent name, a little serial number that never changes, and we keep a ledger of every book it has ever appeared in. If a puzzle ever shows up in a second book, the ledger knows, and so will you, because we will say so. No quiet recycling. Every page you paid for is accounted for.
