Welcome to the very first issue of The Quiet Grid!

Every two weeks, on a Monday, this note brings three squares, two voices,
and one grid. Three small things worth knowing, two lines worth hearing,
and one little puzzle worth trying. That is the whole recipe. It fits in
a coffee break, and we intend to keep it that way. If you would like it
delivered, the signup at the bottom of this page is the quiet way in.

## Three squares

**One.** Start every puzzle by finding one square that can only be one
thing. Not the hardest square, not the cleverest: the one that is simply
obvious. Fill it in, then look again. Somewhere on the grid another square
just became obvious. That gentle chain reaction is the whole game, and it
never gets old.

**Two.** The sound a pencil makes on good paper. Nothing to learn here.
Just a thing worth noticing the next time you sit down with a grid.
Pencil, paper, quiet room. It is one of life's small, reliable pleasures.

**Three.** A peek behind the grid: every page in a Quiet Grid Press book
holds one puzzle and nothing else. No second puzzle crowding in from the
side, no clutter. We decided early that a puzzle deserves the whole page,
the way a good photograph deserves the whole frame.

## Two voices

> And God said, Let there be light: and there was light.
> — Genesis 1:3

That is the first verse in our first book, *Bible Verse Sudoku*, and it
felt like the right place to begin a newsletter, too. First light. First
issue. First square.

The second voice is yours. This issue we have a question instead of a
quotation, because nobody has written in yet. Where does your puzzle time
live? With the morning coffee, stolen from a lunch break, or in the quiet
after dinner? Tell us by replying to the email, and two or three of your
answers will appear here next time.

## One grid

Here is a row from a Sudoku with eight of its nine squares filled:

```
6   7   3   2   8   1   _   9   4
```

Every row holds the digits 1 through 9 exactly once. Which digit belongs
in the empty square? This is the easiest move in all of Sudoku, and also
the most satisfying, because there is no doubt about it at all. Solvers
call it a full house.

The answer, along with a tip of the cap to everyone who wrote in, arrives
in the next issue on September 28.

Happy solving.
