## Three squares

**One.** A naked single is a square that shows you its own answer. Once
you have penciled in the candidates for a few empty squares, look for the
one with only a single small number left in it. That square is not
hiding anything. It is naked, in the polite Sudoku sense of the word, and
it is the next thing you fill in. Train your eye to check the squares
with the most filled neighbors first; that is where naked singles like to
live.

**Two.** Eraser crumbs are proof of thinking, not failure. A clean page
means you never tried anything. A page with a few pink smudges means you
were in the fight. Brush them off with the side of your hand and carry
on. (Onto the floor. We will not tell.)

**Three.** Behind the grid: every puzzle we have ever made has been
checked, independently and by machine, to have exactly one solution. All
610 of them. We ran the sweep again last month for the pleasure of
watching it come back clean. When you finish one of ours, there was only
ever one way it could end, and you found it.

## One grid

The middle box of this grid has four empty squares, marked a, b, c, and
d, and we have penciled the candidates in for you:

```
.  2  . | .  7  . | .  .  .
.  8  9 | .  .  1 | 4  .  6
1  4  . | .  9  . | .  .  .
--------+---------+--------
.  .  4 | 7  5  3 | .  6  .
6  .  5 | 8  a  b | 3  9  7
.  .  . | c  d  9 | .  .  4
--------+---------+--------
.  .  . | 1  .  . | 6  .  .
.  .  . | .  6  5 | .  .  .
.  .  . | .  .  4 | .  7  2
```

Square a can be 1, 2, or 4. Square b can be 2. Square c can be 2 or 6.
Square d can be 1 or 2. One of those four squares has a single candidate.
Which square is it, and what goes there? Say it out loud if you like. It
is very satisfying.
